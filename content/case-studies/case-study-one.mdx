## Context

At SOULAX, I designed the interface for an AI-driven trailer inventory product. It supports operations teams who need to locate trailers across yards, keep inventory current, and complete loading, unloading, and audit tasks from both desktop and mobile screens.

## The challenge

Trailer status changes throughout an operation. Teams need to distinguish available inventory from trailers that are loaded or in progress, find a unit by its ID or location, and understand where it was last recorded. On the floor, staff also need a practical way to identify a trailer and record pallet or load activity without returning to a desktop.

The product goal was to use AI-driven tracking to reduce the manual effort spent searching for trailers. That made findability and clear status especially important: a large inventory is only useful when people can quickly narrow it to the trailer they need.

## My role

As a Specialist UX Designer, I designed the product interface for trailer tracking and inventory management, covering the desktop dashboard and mobile operational flows.

## Designing across desk and yard

The dashboard brings search, location selection, status filters, and a trailer list alongside a map. Each row gives the operator a quick scan of trailer ID, bill of lading, age or status, and last known location. This makes the list useful for both locating a specific unit and reviewing the state of a yard.

The mobile screens focus on the steps that happen away from a desk: entering or scanning a trailer ID, loading and unloading, capturing a bill of lading, reviewing a pallet list, and completing a manual trailer audit. Status labels and confirmation screens help make the current step explicit as a task moves forward.

## Outcome

The design brings real-time, AI-driven trailer tracking together with the desktop search and mobile load, unload, and audit tasks needed to manage inventory. This supports the product goal of reducing the manual effort spent locating trailers.

## Reflection

Inventory visibility has to travel with the work. The map helps teams understand where trailers are, while the mobile flows support the people capturing what happens to them next. Designing both views as parts of the same workflow keeps location and task status connected.
