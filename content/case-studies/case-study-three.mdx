## Context

At SOULAX, I designed an interface for lien registration and search used by financial institutions. The work addresses a document-heavy process where teams need to find records, review their status, and move requests toward a decision.

## The challenge

A lien record can pass through several meaningful states: it may need an initial search, review, correction, approval, or a related waiver and payout request. A workflow that hides those distinctions makes it harder for staff to tell what needs attention and what action comes next.

Search also has to work with the identifiers and source material people have at hand. The screens support looking up records by VIN, entering one or multiple VINs, and uploading a registration document.

## My role

As a Specialist UX Designer, I designed the interface for lien registration and search, with a focus on bringing record discovery and status review into a coherent workflow.

## Making status actionable

The summary dashboard separates work into columns such as “Lien Search To Do,” “Lien Summary To Review,” “Lien Summary To Correct,” “Lien Summary Approved,” and “Waiver & Payout Request.” This board-like structure gives each stage its own place, while the count above a column helps users see where records are grouped.

For a new search, the entry panel offers a choice between a single VIN and multiple VINs, plus registration-PDF upload or direct VIN entry. Once a record is in progress, the card surfaces key details—including VIN, bank, amounts, contact, and validity date—so a reviewer can scan the case without opening every item.

Generated waiver documents continue that path from review to action: the document can be checked, edited, and downloaded from the workflow.

## Outcome

The design supports the goal of making lien searches more efficient and less error-prone by clarifying record status, offering practical search inputs, and keeping generated documents connected to the work.

## Reflection

For regulated or financially consequential work, clarity is not decoration. A visible status, a usable search path, and access to the relevant document help people understand the state of a case before they act on it.
