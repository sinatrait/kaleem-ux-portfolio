## Context

At SOULAX, I designed predictive analytics dashboards for fleet operations. The work translates forecasting models and operational signals into views that help teams move from reacting to problems toward planning maintenance proactively.

## The challenge

Fleet data is useful only when it helps someone make a decision. A manager may need a high-level view of shipment volume and issues, then need to understand which trailer or trip is behind an alert and what has happened along its route.

The product also combines several kinds of information: shipment status, dwell and timing, tractor and trailer activity, temperature concerns, and events recorded by devices or drivers. Presenting all of it with equal emphasis would make it harder to find the signal.

## My role

As a Specialist UX Designer, I designed dashboards and operational workflows that connect predictive information with fleet and shipment activity.

## From signal to context

The dashboard opens with operational summaries and an assistant-oriented entry point, while reports such as current shipments, time-critical transactions, excess trailer dwell, and utilization remain available in the navigation. Alerts can call out conditions that need attention, such as a trailer loaded beyond a time threshold.

For investigation, the shipment view pairs a list of active trips with route and journey details. A timeline can bring planned, device-recorded, and driver-entered events together, making discrepancies easier to spot in context. Views for maps, geofences, and trailer utilization extend the same idea from one trip to broader fleet patterns.

## Outcome

The dashboard direction connects forecasting and fleet signals to shipment-level context, supporting the stated goal of more proactive maintenance planning. The resume does not provide measured maintenance or business results.

## Reflection

Predictive information needs a path to action. Pairing summary signals with the shipment, route, and event details behind them gives operations teams a way to move from “something needs attention” toward understanding where to look next.
