## Context

VisionPulse brings day-to-day service operations into a single web platform. The work shown here covers several connected parts of that system: customer accounts, work orders, assets, service locations, provider profiles, and the settings that keep operational data consistent.

## The challenge

An operations team may start with a customer question and need to check an account, its work orders, and the assets or locations attached to it. Those records are related, but each has its own fields and administrative actions. The experience needed to make it straightforward to find the right record, understand its current state, and continue with the relevant task.

The asset screens add another layer: teams work with serial numbers, service status, customer locations, products, and installation dates. Bulk import and configurable fields also have to fit into the same environment without obscuring routine search and review.

## My role

I worked on the UX and product design for these service-provider workflows, shaping the account, asset, location, and profile experiences shown in the product screens.

## Design focus

I treated record discovery and record management as related, but distinct, tasks. In the customer workspace, accounts, work orders, and assets are available as clear tabs, with search, filters, recent searches, and bulk upload close to the list they affect. This gives the workspace a consistent starting point whether someone is locating one record or adding a group of customers.

For assets, a structured table makes identifying information and operational state scannable together: asset name, serial number, status, customer location, product, and key dates. Location entry uses a dedicated form for address, provider, segment, routes, and related operational attributes, while shared picklists help keep repeated values organized.

## Solution

The resulting screens connect common service operations without collapsing every task into one oversized form. Customer search, asset review, location management, provider profiles, and configuration each have a recognizable home, while the shared navigation keeps them part of one platform.

## What the design supports

The delivered interface covers the service-provider workspace from customer and asset lookup through location and profile administration. Search, table structure, status labels, and dedicated forms give each task a clear entry point, while shared navigation connects the work across the platform.

## Reflection

In an operations product, small data relationships carry real weight: an asset belongs somewhere, a location connects to a provider, and a customer record may sit beside its work orders. Making those relationships visible in the everyday interface is as important as making each individual screen easy to use.
