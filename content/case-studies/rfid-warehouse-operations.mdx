## Context

This mobile logistics app supports warehouse work across shipping, receiving, inventory, and pallet tracking. The screens show an IRIS-branded tool used around scan-driven tasks and operator workflows.

## The challenge

Operators faced confusing scanning flows, limited flexibility when a scan was not possible, and a lack of timely insight into prior activity. In a warehouse, these issues can interrupt a task and make it harder to know whether an item has been recorded correctly.

## My role

I designed key mobile workflows and improved the interaction patterns around scanning, manual entry, task feedback, and history.

## Designing for scanning and exceptions

I audited the existing UX, gathered stakeholder input, and prototyped clearer task flows. The updated experience gives scanning a prominent place, while offering manual entry as an alternate path when needed.

Contextual feedback and visual guidance help operators understand what the app expects and what has happened. A history view gives users a way to review past activity rather than relying only on the current scan.

## Solution

The resulting mobile flows cover shipping, receiving, inventory, and pallet tracking. They combine scan-led interactions with manual overrides, task status, and a recent-history view.

## Outcomes

The portfolio describes increased scanning success, fewer errors, faster task completion, and improved operator satisfaction. No numerical measurements are supplied for these outcomes.

## Reflection

Scanning is only one part of a warehouse task. Designing the fallback, confirmation, and history states makes the workflow more resilient when a scan does not go as expected.
