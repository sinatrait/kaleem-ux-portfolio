Overview

Project Overview

Enterprise financial quoting is notoriously painful. A major B2B services company was losing deals and frustrating their sales teams with a quoting process that required navigating 7 different systems, manual data entry across spreadsheets, and up to 3 days of back-and-forth to generate a single quote.

The company had invested in AI models capable of automatically pricing complex service packages. The question was: how do you design an interface that makes AI-generated quotes feel trustworthy, editable, and fast?

"Our reps were spending more time building the quote than actually selling. We needed to flip that ratio entirely."

Problem

The Problem Space

The existing quoting process was a patchwork of legacy tools that had grown organically over a decade. Sales reps had to gather inputs from five internal teams before even starting a quote, and the final document was assembled manually in a Word template.

Process fragmentation

7 systems required. No single source of truth for pricing, scope, or approvals.

Quote errors & rework

Manual entry led to frequent pricing errors, requiring 2+ revision cycles per quote on average.

AI not trusted

An earlier AI pricing tool had been built but wasn't adopted — reps felt they couldn't understand or verify the AI's logic.

Approval bottlenecks

Complex quotes required sign-off from 3 departments, with no visibility into where a quote was stuck.

UX Approach

Research & UX Approach

I ran a three-week discovery phase that included: job-shadowing sessions with sales reps, stakeholder interviews with sales ops and finance leadership, and a review audit of 40+ existing quotes to map the range of configurations.

The most significant research finding was that the previous AI tool had failed not because of model quality, but because of a UX problem: the AI made recommendations but never explained its reasoning. Reps felt they were being asked to sign off on a black box.

Service blueprint mapping the existing 7-system quoting workflow

This led to the central design thesis: AI-assisted, human-confirmed. The system should do the heavy lifting — but always give reps the visibility and control to understand, adjust, and own the final quote.

Design Process

Design Process

I structured the design work in two parallel tracks: one focused on the information architecture and guided workflow; the other on the AI output presentation — specifically, how to make AI pricing feel legible and trustworthy without burdening users with model internals.

◧ Wireframes — Guided Input Flow
Early wireframes of the guided quote input wizard

I designed and tested three different interaction models for how the AI surfaces its recommendations: a "confidence band" model, a structured breakdown model, and a side-by-side comparison model. User testing with sales reps clearly favored the structured breakdown — they wanted to understand the "why" behind each line item.

Key Decisions

Key Design Decisions

Guided wizard structure

The complex multi-system input was consolidated into a 5-step guided wizard, reducing cognitive load and eliminating the need to switch tools.

Explainable AI pricing

Each AI-generated line item includes an expandable "Why this price?" panel with plain-language reasoning drawn from comparable quotes and pricing rules.

Inline negotiation mode

Reps can adjust any line item with the AI recalculating dependencies in real time, surfacing risk flags if a change breaks margin thresholds.

Approval tracking dashboard

A dedicated view shows where each quote sits in the approval chain, with one-click escalation for urgent deals.

Solution

Final Solution

The platform replaced 7 disconnected systems with a single, end-to-end quoting experience. The AI generates a complete draft quote from basic deal parameters in under 60 seconds — which the sales rep then reviews, adjusts, and submits for approval within the same interface.
