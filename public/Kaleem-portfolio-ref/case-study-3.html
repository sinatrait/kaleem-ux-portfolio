AI Trailer Inventory System

Designing a real-time inventory management system that gives logistics operators full visibility and control over thousands of trailers across a distributed network — without the chaos.

Overview

Project Overview

One of the largest logistics operators in North America was managing a fleet of over 8,000 trailers across 120+ distribution sites — with no reliable, real-time view of where each trailer was, what condition it was in, or whether it was available for dispatch.

The company had invested in AI-powered sensor technology that could automatically detect trailer location, condition status, and utilization. The design challenge: how do you build an interface that makes this torrent of real-time data usable for operations managers whose priority is moving cargo — not interpreting dashboards?

"We had the sensors. We had the data. What we didn't have was a way for our people to actually use it to make decisions."

Problem

The Problem Space

The legacy system was a spreadsheet-based inventory tracker updated manually twice a day. With a fleet of this scale, the data was almost always outdated by the time it was used. Operations managers were making dispatch decisions based on information that could be 12+ hours old.

Stale, manual data

Trailer status updated twice daily by hand. Location, condition, and availability were frequently inaccurate.

No cross-site visibility

Each site managed its own local spreadsheet — no aggregated view of fleet availability across the network.

Reactive maintenance

Without condition monitoring, maintenance was addressed only after failures — causing costly downtime and dispatch delays.

Data volume overwhelm

Sensor data arrived continuously, but there was no logic to prioritize what required action versus routine status updates.

UX Approach

Research & UX Approach

I spent two weeks embedded with operations teams at two distribution sites, observing how dispatch decisions were actually made in the flow of a work shift. I also interviewed yard managers, fleet coordinators, and regional operations leads to understand the differences in how each role related to inventory data.

The core insight was role divergence: yard managers needed a granular, site-level view to coordinate day-to-day dispatch; regional coordinators needed a 30,000-foot view to balance utilization across sites. A single interface couldn't serve both — so I designed a role-adaptive system with shared data but distinct views.

Real-time over comprehensive

Show the current moment clearly. Historical depth is secondary to operational immediacy.

Exception-first design

The system should surface what needs attention — not make users scan everything to find problems.

Role-adaptive views

Same data, different interfaces for site-level and network-level operators.

Dispatch-native actions

Inventory management actions should align with the actual language and workflow of dispatch operations.

Design Process

Design Process

The design process was shaped heavily by the real-time data challenge. Early in discovery, I ran a data sprint with engineering and data science to understand what the sensor system could reliably provide — and at what latency. This determined the information architecture before a single wireframe was drawn.

◧ Information Architecture Diagram
Information architecture map showing data flows and view hierarchy

I prototyped the yard-level map view through four iterations, each tested with site managers in a lab setting. The key challenge was density: a 500-trailer yard needed to be navigable on a single screen without losing individual asset detail. The solution was a dynamic zoom model with cluster abstraction at low zoom and individual trailer cards at high zoom.

Key Decisions

Key Design Decisions

Dynamic map with live status

Trailers are represented as live markers on a spatial map, colored by availability and condition status — immediately communicating what's dispatachable.

AI-predictive maintenance alerts

Instead of only flagging current failures, the AI surfaces trailers likely to need maintenance within 72 hours — enabling proactive scheduling.

Site-vs-network toggle

A persistent toggle switches between yard-level granularity and regional fleet view — same UI, different data scope.

Dispatch integration

Trailer assignment can be initiated directly from the inventory system, with a one-click handoff to the dispatch platform — eliminating a manual handoff step.

Solution

Final Solution

The final system delivers a live, role-adaptive view of the entire trailer fleet — updated every 60 seconds from sensor data. Operations managers at every level now have a single source of truth that reflects the actual state of their inventory, not yesterday's spreadsheet entry.
